Antalgic-Trac® Spinal Decompression in Lighthouse Point

When a disc herniates or bulges, the nerve roots around your spine can become compressed, triggering pain that radiates down your leg or arm. Antalgic-Trac® is a computerized spinal decompression system that gently stretches the spinal column, creating negative pressure within the discs. This process can help reduce inflammation and allow herniated disc material to move back into place—without surgery or medication.

How Antalgic-Trac® Works

The system applies controlled, progressive distraction to decompress the spinal column. Over a series of sessions, the gentle pulling motion relieves pressure on nerves and encourages the intervertebral discs to rehydrate. Patients typically experience reduced pain and improved mobility. The therapy is non-invasive, painless, and often allows people to avoid or delay more aggressive interventions.

Who Benefits From This Treatment

Antalgic-Trac® may help patients with sciatica, chronic lower back pain, neck pain, and conditions related to herniated or bulging discs. It works best when combined with chiropractic adjustments, therapeutic massage, and postural correction—a whole-system approach rather than a quick fix.

Common Questions

Is seeing a chiropractor worth it?
That depends on your condition and expectations. Chiropractic care can provide real relief from acute and chronic pain, especially when paired with corrective therapies like spinal decompression. However, results vary. Dr. McNamara-Krauss will tell you honestly whether she believes she can help you, rather than promising a cure. Some patients find sustained improvement; others benefit from ongoing maintenance care.

What is better, an osteopath or a chiropractor?
Osteopaths (DOs) are fully licensed physicians trained in whole-body medicine and can prescribe medications. Chiropractors focus specifically on spinal alignment, joint health, and nervous system function through adjustments and specialized therapies. Neither is universally "better"—it depends on your symptoms and whether you need primary medical care or targeted spine and joint treatment.

Should you massage out a pinched nerve?
Light massage on the soft tissues surrounding a pinched nerve can help by relaxing tight muscles and boosting blood flow. However, avoid direct pressure on the nerve itself, and stop if pain worsens. If numbness, weakness, or structural issues like a bulged disc are involved, you need professional evaluation—not just massage.
